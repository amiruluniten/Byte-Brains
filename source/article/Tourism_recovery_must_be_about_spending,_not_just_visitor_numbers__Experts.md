## **Malaysia’s tourism recovery risks becoming a numbers game, experts warn**  

**PETALING JAYA**: Malaysia’s tourism recovery risks becoming a numbers game if rising visitor arrivals are not matched by higher spending and longer stays, experts said.

UiTM Industry, Community, Alumni and Entrepreneurship Relations Division deputy rector Prof Dr Mohd Hafiz Hanafiah said arrivals measured volume rather than the sector’s economic performance and should be assessed alongside tourism receipts, average per-capita expenditure and duration of stay.

He added that the figure also included same-day visitors who did not stay overnight, while the sector’s true economic value was reflected in the yield generated by each traveller.

“If arrivals increase by 11% but length of stay and per-capita expenditure remain stagnant or decline, the growth is extensive (more visitors) rather than intensive, with more value generated by each person.

“The impact is that supporting costs involving infrastructure, environmental pressure and congestion may rise faster than the returns from tourism activities,” he told theSun.

Hafiz was referring to Tourism, Arts and Culture Ministry figures presented to the Cabinet and announced by Communications Minister Datuk Fahmi Fadzil on Feb 27, which showed Malaysia recorded 42.19 million international visitor arrivals in 2025, an 11.2% increase from 37.96 million in 2024.

He also said Malaysia should preserve its strong regional visitor base while diversifying towards markets capable of generating greater value from each visit.

“The aim is not to reject regional markets, which remain important for visitor volume and resilience, but to broaden the market mix to include long-haul and higherspending segments from the Middle East, premium segments in China, Europe and Oceania.

“A large proportion of arrivals from Singapore enter through the Johor-Singapore land crossings, which naturally generate high rates of day trips, repeat visits and short stays.

“We must remember that Thailand’s arrivals are more flightbased, involving longer distances, higher yields and longer stays, while part of Malaysia’s numerical advantage is supported by convenient land-border traffic that generates lower per-capita value.”

Hafiz said Malaysia should shift from pursuing quantity to securing stronger returns from each visit, while retaining regional travellers who continued to provide a dependable base for the tourism industry.

The ministry stated that Asean contributed 72.8% of Malaysia’s international visitor arrivals during the period, led by Singapore, while Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar recorded strong increases.

It attributed the performance to Visit Malaysia 2026 promotions, improved regional connectivity, international events and wider promotion of tourism products.

Meanwhile, Universiti Utara Malaysia international strategic management expert Assoc Prof Dr Narentheren Kaliappen said the strong recovery provided Malaysia with a solid foundation to make each visit more valuable and spread tourism benefits beyond established centres.

“The 21 million is very good news and shows tourism has bounced back well. However, the number of visitors is only half the story. What matters just as much is how much they spend and how long they stay.

“The real question is: now that many people are coming, how do we make each visit worth more?”

Narentheren proposed packaging destinations together to encourage travellers to venture beyond Kuala Lumpur to places such as Ipoh, Malacca, Sabah and Sarawak, while using events, festivals, food and shopping to turn brief trips into longer holidays.

He added that Malaysia could also expand higher-value segments such as medical, eco, halal and business tourism, supported by better direct-flight connections and simpler visa arrangements.

To distribute the gains more widely, he called for improved road, rail and air access to smaller destinations, a nationwide spread of events and stronger support for homestays, village tourism operators and local guides.

“The next goal is to make each visit count for more and ensure the benefits reach beyond the big cities.

“Get that right, and we do not just recover our numbers – we build a stronger and fairer tourism economy.
