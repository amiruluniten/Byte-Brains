# 7 · Judge Q&A defence — the five C1 answers

Anticipated judge questions and the agreed answers. Use these word-for-word in the
report's Methodology section and in live Q&A. The C4 product answers live in
`6-impact-app-concept.md`.

## "Does this project relate to any SDG?" (KDH4)

Primary: **SDG 8.9** — "devise and implement policies to promote sustainable tourism
that creates jobs and promotes local culture and products." Our yield lens is exactly a
tourism policy measure: it tells policy which source markets create value.
Supporting: **SDG 17.18** — better data supports better decisions. The Volume Trap is a
measurement critique: arrival-count KPIs reward low-yield same-day traffic, so a better
measure (tourism yield) changes what policy optimises.

## "Why is 2019 the anchor for the Missing Billions?" (KDH3)

2019 is the **official pre-crisis baseline** — the standard reference year for
post-pandemic recovery comparisons. It is a **measuring stick, not a target**: the
finding is the stagnation line (2024 real per-visitor tourism yield = 2019, constant
2019 prices), not a promise to return to 2019. We measure against 2019; we do not
recommend reverting to it.

## "Why exactly four clusters in the segmentation?" (KDH3)

k=4 is a **policy choice**, not a statistical one: each cluster maps to exactly one
prescription — invest in **High-Yield Long-Haul**, scale **High-Growth Emerging**,
monitor **Low-Yield Steady**, fix the **Volume Traps**. Four segments, four actions;
more clusters would split markets without producing a new action. Determinism (fixed
seeds, best-of-12 restarts) guarantees the grouping is stable. The assignment is
evidence-based: every market's segment follows from its measured traits.

## "Is the RM10.1 billion from lower yield, or just the market mix shifting?" (KDH5)

Both, blended — and we say so. The counterfactual uses the **national average
per-visitor yield**, so it combines the change in each source market's yield with the
change in the market mix. Separating the two effects is future work. This is stated in
the Scope & limits section (`2-method-notes.md` §2.7). Example on request: tourists who
still spend like 2019 tourists plus more low-yield same-day visitors pull the national
average down with no individual change — the counterfactual counts both.

## "What are the study's limits?" (KDH2)

See `2-method-notes.md` §2.7: inbound visitors only; 2019–2025 window (2025
preliminary); top-20 source markets (four excluded with stated reasons); constant
2019 prices only; a single national CPI deflator; and the blended yield/mix
limitation above. Every limit is declared, none discovered by a judge.
