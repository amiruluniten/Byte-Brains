# Regional yield benchmark 2024 — Thailand, Indonesia, Vietnam (vs Malaysia)

*Researched: 2026-09-13. Fact-finding for the regional receipts-per-visitor comparison.
All 2024 figures are full-calendar-year, from each country's official tourism authority /
statistical office unless marked otherwise. USD conversions noted per figure.*

### Per-visitor yield vs Malaysia and vs 2019 WDI baseline

2019 WDI (World Bank `ST.INT.RCPT.CD` ÷ arrivals): Malaysia $851, Thailand $1,613,
Indonesia $1,143, Philippines $1,387. Note: WDI receipts for these countries stop at
2020 in the current WDI build, so 2019 remains the latest WDI-comparable yield.

| Country | 2024 yield (US$) | 2019 WDI yield (US$) | 2024 vs 2019 | 2024 yield vs Malaysia 2024 ($600) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Malaysia | 600 | 851 | −29% | — |
| Thailand | ~1,363 | 1,613 | −16% | **2.3× Malaysia** |
| Indonesia | ~1,202 | 1,143 | +5% | **2.0× Malaysia** |
| Vietnam | n/a (revenue incl. domestic) | n/a (no clean WDI 2024) | — | ⚠ naive 840T VND ÷ 17.5M = ~$1,889 but this is **invalid** — numerator includes domestic tourism |

Key takeaways:
1. Malaysia's yield problem is real and regional: Thailand and Indonesia both earn roughly
   2.0–2.3× more foreign receipts per visitor in 2024, while Malaysia's own yield fell ~29%
   below its 2019 level.
2. Thailand's 2024 yield (~$1,363) is still ~16% below its 2019 survey-based yield ($1,613).
3. Indonesia's 2024 yield ($1,202) has just recovered past its 2019 WDI level ($1,143) — but
   the two figures are not methodologically identical (2019 WDI = BOP travel credit; 2024 = same
   BI BOP basis, so this comparison is actually the cleanest one in the table).

## Country detail

### Thailand (MOTS — Ministry of Tourism and Sports)

- **2024 international arrivals: 35,546,072** (+26.27% y/y). Official MOTS figure.
- **2024 international tourism revenue: ฿1.67 trillion ≈ US$48.45 bn** (MOTS statement,
  Jan 2025; Xinhua's stated conversion ≈ 34.5 THB/USD). +34% y/y.
  - Methodology note: MOTS's own year-end 2025 summary (released 1 Jan 2026) restates
    2024 as the base for "revenue from international tourists ฿1,536,574m, down 4.71%",
    implying a restated 2024 figure of ~฿1.61 trillion. The 1.67T vs 1.61T gap is a known
    revision/rounding artifact in MOTS releases; 1.67T is the widely quoted headline number.
  - Computed per-visitor: ฿1.67T ÷ 35.55m ≈ ฿46,987 ≈ **US$1,363**.
- **2025 full-year: PUBLISHED.** MOTS year-end summary (1 Jan 2026):
  - Arrivals: **32,974,321** (−7.23%)
  - International tourism revenue: **฿1,536,574 million** (−4.71%) ≈ US$46–49 bn
    (2025 average rate ~32.7–33.3 THB/USD; govt PRD release quotes US$86.49 bn for the
    ฿2.70T combined total using ~31.25)
  - Domestic: 202.37m trips, ฿1,166,761m. Combined total: ฿2,703,335m (−1.26%).
  - 2025 per-visitor: ฿1,536,574m ÷ 32.97m ≈ ฿46,596 ≈ US$1,400–1,420.
- Receipts basis: **survey-based** — MOTS runs a continuous foreign-tourist expenditure
  survey (departure-point sampling); the revenue figures are survey estimates, not
  balance-of-payments. Explains the gap vs Bank of Thailand BOP travel credit.
- Sources:
  - Xinhua summary of MOTS statement: https://english.news.cn/20250107/557d03a80370448b895fbad449e33203/c.html
  - TPNNational (MOTS 31 Jan 2025 statement): https://tpnnational.com/2025/02/01/thailands-foreign-arrivals-surge-26-27-percent-in-2024-generating-1-67-trillion-baht/
  - Nation Thailand (MOTS year-end 2025 summary, 1 Jan 2026): https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40060629
  - Official portal: https://www.mots.go.th/

- **2024 tourism foreign exchange: US$16.71 bn** (angka sementara/preliminary).

  - Ministry-stated average spend per foreign visitor 2025: **US$1,267** (target US$1,220).

### Vietnam (GSO / VNAT)

- **2024 international arrivals: 17.5 million** (+39.5% y/y). Official GSO figure
  (14.8m by air = 84.4%). GSO publishes the rounded headline number.
- **2024 tourism revenue: ~840 trillion VND ≈ US$32.96 bn** (estimated, +23% y/y).
  **Caveat: this is TOTAL tourism revenue (international + domestic)**, published by the
  Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism / VNAT as an administrative aggregate of
  provincial/sector estimates. **Vietnam does not publish a clean international-only
  receipts figure** (its WDI BOP receipts series is also stale/absent post-2020 in the
  current WDI build). Therefore a defensible 2024 receipts-per-visitor yield cannot be
  computed; the naive 840T ÷ 17.5m ≈ US$1,889 overstates the international yield because
  domestic revenue is in the numerator. 135m domestic trips in 2024 make domestic revenue
  a large share.
- **2025 full-year: PUBLISHED (preliminary VNAT/NTA estimates, 30 Dec 2025).**
  - Arrivals: **~22 million** international (first time above the 2019 record of 18.0m;
    ~110% of pre-pandemic recovery)
  - Revenue: **~1,000 trillion VND** total (incl. domestic), +22.1% y/y; 135m domestic trips.

  - VNAT site (arrivals): https://vietnamtourism.gov.vn/en/post/20764
  - vietnam.vn (2025 record, revenue ~1 quadrillion VND): https://www.vietnam.vn/en/du-lich-viet-nam-2025-ky-luc-22-trieu-luot-khach-quoc-te

## Methodology caveats (read before comparing)

1. **Survey vs administrative**: Thailand = visitor expenditure survey; Indonesia = Bank
   Indonesia BOP travel credit (administrative); Vietnam = administrative revenue aggregate
   (includes domestic); Malaysia = visitor expenditure survey. Cross-country yield gaps of
   10–20% can be methodological, not real.
2. **Vietnam is not comparable** for receipts per visitor — no international-only receipts
   published. Use arrivals and total revenue with an explicit caveat.
3. **Thailand's 1.67T vs restated ~1.61T** for 2024 international revenue: cite 1.67T
   (headline) and flag the restatement.
4. **Exchange rates**: 2024 averages used — THB ~34.5, IDR ~15,869, VND ~25,400, MYR ~4.68
   per USD. MOTS/VNAT-stated USD conversions used where published.
5. WDI (World Bank) receipts for THA/IDN/MYS/PHL/VNM end at 2020 in the current build
   (checked 2026-09-13 via API), so 2019 remains the latest WDI yield benchmark.
